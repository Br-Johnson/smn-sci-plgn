# Scientific framing and limits

Bull et al. (2022), *The likely suspects framework: the need for a life cycle
approach for managing Atlantic salmon stocks across multiple scales*, describes
organising hypotheses, indicators and evidence across life stages and management
scales. It motivates this proposal workflow; it does not supply a validated
Pacific salmon model or automatically partition mortality.

- Primary paper: https://doi.org/10.1093/icesjms/fsac099
- Programme description: https://atlanticsalmontrust.org/our-work/suspects-framework/

BECI's science plan describes federated discovery and shared synthesis across
climate, ecosystem and fisheries sources while respecting data ownership.
Connecting a local evidence ledger follows that design principle; this plugin
does not claim BECI affiliation or delivery of the whole programme.

- Primary resources: https://beci.info/resources/
- Science plan: https://beci.info/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/BECI-Project_science-plan_2026_01_28.pdf

The workbench's earlier analytical-framework requirement describes measurement
models, standardisation templates, comparability and analysis recipes:
https://github.com/Br-Johnson/smn-data-gpt/blob/main/docs/PRODUCT-REQUIREMENTS.md
It is historical design context, not proof of an implemented API.

For a first-winter project, distinguish marine-entry cohort, first-winter age,
stage-specific survival with detection/effort assumptions, sampled encounters,
and modelled ocean context. Evidence gaps and exclusions remain explicit. A
paper or bioRxiv draft must describe only performed checks/results; unrun models
and unscored model evaluations are planned work, never findings.
